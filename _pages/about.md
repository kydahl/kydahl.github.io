## About me

I am an applied mathematician specializing in the use of dynamical systems to study ecological processes, particularly those that drive outbreaks of mosquito-borne diseases. My research integrates mathematical modeling, data analysis, and theoretical approaches to understand how environmental and biological factors shape disease dynamics.

I am currently engaged in projects that explore how temperature variations influence mosquito populations and the transmission potential of vector-borne diseases. My work combines differential equations, stochastic modeling, and parameter estimation techniques to develop predictive models for public health interventions.

## Research Interests

- Mathematical models of mosquito-borne disease transmission
- Dynamical systems approaches to epidemic spread and control
- Trait-based modeling to explore how environmental factors shape vector populations
- Data-driven methods for estimating epidemiological parameters and forecasting outbreaks

## Academic Background

I hold a Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics, where my dissertation focused on mathematical frameworks for studying mosquito-borne disease outbreaks. My research is informed by collaborations with ecologists, epidemiologists, and public health professionals.
